Case study 02 · Fall 2026

The Garden: my portfolio as a product

Designer and product owner

My role
Designer and product owner
Built with
Figma, Clip Studio Paint, HTML, CSS, JavaScript
Status
Live at myeminme.github.io

I drew the art, designed the interactions and content, and directed the build. The code was written with AI-assisted coding.

The problem

A portfolio has two audiences: people who want to enjoy the art, and recruiters who have about a minute.

I wanted a site that feels like my illustrations and still gets people to the work fast.

The idea

The home page is an illustrated garden. The three signs are the menu: About Me, Portfolio, and Contact. On phones the same three options sit as buttons at the bottom of the screen.

Clicking my character zooms in and starts a visual-novel style chat. You can ask me questions, flip through my journal, or take a photo with me.

Design decisions

  • Nothing about a drawing says “click me”, so a small notice tells first-time visitors they can click on my character.
  • The character has calm and excited expressions while chatting. I redrew the smiling face after it came across as creepy.
  • I wireframed the chat in Figma before it was built, then adjusted the layout in Figma and matched the site to it.
  • Contact used to point to Instagram. I moved everything to email and a contact form so inquiries land in one place.
  • A “What are you hiring for?” switch lets recruiters pick a role and see the most relevant work first.

Letting people control the motion

A settings menu in the corner lets visitors turn on lighter animations (on automatically for slower devices), stop the scene from following the mouse, or pause all movement.

The avatar is a set of drawn frames: eyes open or closed, and mouth closed, talking, or grinning. The frames swap as the streamer talks and blinks.

In my portfolio the avatar talks on its own, closes its eyes when you click it, and can react to your microphone.
